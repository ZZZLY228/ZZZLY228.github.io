GoldBench RoadTrip

Driving companion · iPhone

Press record.
Then just drive.

RoadTrip draws the road you are on as you drive it, and keeps the drive afterwards with its distance, its time and the photos you took along the way. The map carries the weather above you and the places beside you — a park, a charger, a restaurant and the inspection record filed for it. There is no account and no server: everything stays on the phone.

01

The road, drawn as you drive it

Start recording and the route grows behind you — in the background, with the screen locked, and after the phone has put the app away. Pause for a long stop and pick it up again, or leave it: a long enough stop closes the trip on its own and the next move starts a new one. Drives too short to mean anything are dropped rather than saved. The line follows the road you actually drove; the wandering a phone does while parked is cleaned off it.

02

What the drive came to

The card at the bottom of the map keeps the distance, the drive time and the average speed for the trip you are on or the one you opened. Average speed counts the time you were actually moving, so red lights, parking and pauses do not quietly drag it down.

03

Every drive you kept

Saved drives are grouped by the day they happened and can be narrowed to today, the past week or the past month, or picked out of a calendar by month and year. Choose a day and the map shows everything you drove that day, framed to fit; choose a single drive and it opens on its own. Deleting is a long press, and what you delete waits in a bin for a month before it is really gone.

04

The day's photos, with the day's drive

Every day you drove gets a folder, and the photos and videos you took while the route was being drawn land in it by themselves — photos by the moment they were taken, videos by the stretch of road they cover. What counts is when something was actually shot, not when it reached the phone, so a clip someone sends you days later still belongs to the drive it came from. Behind the folder is that day's map, marking where each photo was taken and which part of the road a video covers. The originals stay in your photo library: nothing is copied out, and anything you delete there disappears here too.

05

The weather you are driving into

A small badge in the corner of the map carries the wind, the conditions right now, and the next change worth knowing about with the time it is expected — rain starting, rain stopping, cloud giving way. It knows local sunrise and sunset, so the icon is honest about whether it is day or night where you are, and it refreshes as you cover ground.

06

What's beside the road

Search for a store or a place, or just tap a pin. The card that comes up tells you about it — and for a restaurant that includes the health inspection record filed for it, so you can decide before you pull in. Press GO and the destination opens in the navigation app you prefer.

07

Choose what the map shows you

Parks, fuel, charging, hotels, gyms, groceries and places to eat each have their own layer, and each one is yours to switch on or off. Fuel can be narrowed to the rewards programmes you actually carry. National parks, monuments, forests, recreation areas, seashores and state parks all have their own pins, and a long press marks one as visited — a record that stays with you and survives a restart. Turn a layer off and its pins leave without disturbing anything else.

08

Yours, and only on your phone

There is no sign-up and no account, because there is nowhere for your drives to go. What is yours to set is the picture that stands on the map in place of the plain dot, and the colour your routes are drawn in. A guide inside the app explains the rest.

The rest of it

Smaller things that make the difference on a long day of driving.

A nudge when you forget

Set off without pressing record and the app notices you are driving and says so, once, rather than letting the trip go unrecorded.

A day can hold many drives

Morning run, afternoon errand, evening drive home — each is kept separately under the same day, newest first.

Nothing is really lost

Deleted drives sit in the bin for a month. Trips too short to count are never saved in the first place.

Photos stay where they are

Folders point at your photo library rather than copying it. Edit a picture here and you are editing the real one.

Pick up where you stopped

Pause during a long break and the detail of the route is kept intact; the trip picks up cleanly when you move again.

Check off the parks

Long press a federal site to mark it visited, and again to undo it. The marks are kept per category and stay put.

How to use it

  1. Start the driveOpen the app and press Start Recording. If you forget and set off anyway, it notices you are driving and reminds you.
  2. Let it runLock the phone and drive. Pause it for a long stop, or leave it — a long enough stop closes the trip for you.
  3. Take your photosShoot with the normal camera. Anything captured while the route was being drawn is filed into that day's folder.
  4. Look aroundTap a pin for its card, check a restaurant's inspection record, and press GO to hand the destination to a navigation app.
  5. Come back to itOpen saved drives, pick a day or a single trip, and it returns to the map with its distance and drive time.
  6. Make it yoursChoose which map layers appear, set the colour your routes are drawn in, and put your own picture on the map in place of the dot.
